####### mx promising mx-codes
--- howItWorks
Geometry is the production 'hints' mesh, untouched. Only the material a fragment shows changes. The boundary is reconstructed on the CPU as a contour, stored per surface block, and evaluated per fragment. This is the production-faithful successor of round 1's mx-contour, which computed its codes lazily inside fragment() and so hid the CPU cost. Code: work/mx/prod.mjs (CPU and JS emulation of the GPU), work/mx/gpu/mx.wgsl (fragment side).

CPU, inside mesh() (honest cost):
1. prep: material and 6 exposure bits for every cell of the 52³ region.
2. For each of the 6 directions d, the surface blocks exposed toward d that a fragment of this chunk can find. A block gets a code if a surface-followed in-plane neighbour has another known material. Sharp blocks count as "unknown": they are never cut and never bleed.
3. Cracks (unit edges between two surface blocks of different material) belong to the block on their low side. Each crack's crossing τ is fitted exactly once, in a 15×15 surface-followed window centred on its owner (axis range ±2). If the owner's window sees a different neighbour (overhangs, cliffs), the fit falls back to the high cell's window. Both are canonical, so neighbouring blocks and neighbouring chunks get bit-identical τ.
   - Topology inside the window: xBR saddle weights decide which diagonal connects. Connect on a margin ≥ 2 with weight ≥ 3. Otherwise follow weaker evidence, unless the saddle belongs to an extended checker (≥ 12 connected saddle points within 7×7), in which case pin it.
   - Corners are kept where two runs ≥ 3 meet. A chain made of one cell's edges stays square.
   - The fit is a TLS line over the digital-straight run window (≥ 9 cracks), blended over 4 cracks into a weighted parabola over ±6 cracks. A crossing may only slide along its dual edge, |τ| ≤ 0.45, so every block centre keeps its own material.
4. A coded block's boundary pieces come from the links at its 4 corners and the τ table. Pieces are clipped to the cell; edge hits are computed in a canonical edge frame, so the two cells sharing an edge quantise the same point. They are joined into ≤ 2 passages and packed into 64 bits. A passage is entry and exit on the perimeter (6+6 bits at 1/16), an optional interior point (5+5 bits at 1/32), and the materials on its left and right (3+3 bits). A lone block becomes a disc code.
5. Texel volume: 38³ r32uint per chunk (material, sharp, exposure, has-code mask, code base) plus array<vec2u> codes, about 171 per chunk.
6. Optional per-vertex skip bit (flags bit 2): set when an exact conservative test proves every fragment of every triangle at that vertex shows its own material without touching a code.

GPU (fragment):
1. If the interpolated skip bit is 1, show the own material with no loads.
2. Otherwise choose the projection axis: y while |n_y| ≥ 0.5·max(|n_x|, |n_z|) (about 63°), else the dominant horizontal axis.
3. The column is floor of the in-plane position. The surface block is searched within ±2 levels of the fragment's level (1–3 texel loads).
4. If that block has a code for this axis, load 8 bytes and run ≤ 2 two-segment polyline side tests. Otherwise show the block's material.

The WGSL version compiles in Dawn (the repo's `webgpu` package; llvmpipe here) and equals the JS emulation on 450,000 samples.

--- doesNotFix
 - Surface jagginess (terrace bands, facets, AO stripes) is untouched: geometry is the hints mesh. That part belongs to the geometry and AO teams (gn-best, gs-ctx/gs-mls, gh-cc8).
 - Low-frequency lumpiness inherited from block-centre rasterisation. Discs score 0.111 / 0.130 / 0.163 for r 2.4 / 4.3 / 7.6, while mf-iso scores 0.130 / 0.129 / 0.148, so it is better on the two larger discs. Small flats and notches are visible on the r 7.6 disc (sheet plans-a).
 - The 2-wide diagonal band scores 0.017 (mf-iso 0.000), and the 1:3 edge 0.013 (mf-iso 0.007).
 - Anti-aliasing: the boundary runs inside triangles, so 4× MSAA does not smooth it. The code gives an exact distance for an fwidth blend, but that is not implemented.
 - Very shallow slopes beyond about 1:10: a 15-wide window cannot see two steps, so these get gentle waves.
 - On slopes under about 63° the projection shows the column-top material on step risers, so dirt exposed on terrace sides disappears where hints and mf-iso show it. This is a deliberate look change, not a fix.
--- failureModes
 - Projection-axis switch. Material images from different axes disagree, so the switch curve can tear. With max-component switching at 45°, a dirt patch on the mountain was cut by a straight edge (work/mx/dbg/mtn-zoom-mat.png). The y-bias (y up to about 63°) removes this on terrain slopes. It remains on mixed-material cliffs above 63° and on the walls of builds.
 - Asymmetric surface following at overhangs, cliffs and built walls: a crack's owner window and its high cell's window both see a different neighbour, and τ falls back to 0. This happens about 6.4 times per chunk on average, mostly in 3 of 16 mountain chunks, and gives small local kinks.
 - Column search misses (no surface within ±2 levels): 0.66% of mountain-view fragments, on cave undersides and at house wall feet. They keep the quad's own material, which matched in every case seen.
 - Generator 'dither' (checker-like grass/dirt alternation on steep slopes). Small groups connect into a ring or patch with tiny grass holes, where mf-iso makes one blob (sheet hills). Groups of 12 or more saddle points stay a crisp checker. So a painted checker of smooth blocks smaller than about 4×4 gets connected; sharp blocks are the opt-out.
 - Heuristic look choices: lone blocks become discs (area ×0.79), 1-wide line ends taper to points (brush strokes), and corners where two runs ≥ 3 meet stay crisp. On generated terrain the last one keeps 3×3 or larger rectangular patches square-cornered.
 - Cells crossed by more than 2 passages (0.13 per chunk) drop the least important one, a small local error.
 - Chunk-border consistency rests on the region apron. The dependency bound is ±8 in-plane and ±5 along the axis (Wa 2), coded box in-plane [−1, 32] and axis [−3, 34], so 52³ is required for this layout. With Wa 4, aprons 9 / 8 / 6 produced 1 / 42 / 115 mismatches. No search touched the region edge in 30 real chunks.
 - Cost: the unedited streaming path must generate 52³ instead of 38³ (+4.3 ms), plus about 6 ms of codes and skip bits per chunk. The GPU cost is unmeasured on the floor iGPU (see production).
 - Tuning was done on 16 terrain chunks, gh's 4 edit scenarios and the synthetic scenes: corner run 3, minLine 9, blend 4, saddle margins, checker group 12, y-bias 0.5.
--- openQuestions
 - Real-GPU cost on the i7-1255U at 2.1 MP with 4× MSAA: the estimate is +0.3–0.7 ms per frame and was not measured.
 - Is about +115% worker time per streamed chunk acceptable (52³ generation plus codes)? The alternatives are building the global heightfield image pass and the apron-9 layout B first, or limiting mx to edited regions.
 - Owner taste: crisp square corners and checkers that follow the blocks (mx) versus rounding everything (mf-iso); lone blocks as discs; tapered line ends; the plan projection hiding exposed dirt on terrace risers; generator dither as small connected patches.
 - Implement and validate layout B: 16-bit own-chunk texels, a code hash and neighbour-slot lookups, 14–27 MiB at R = 192 instead of 42–84 MiB.
 - Anti-aliasing of the in-triangle boundary: an analytic distance plus an fwidth blend would need a second albedo sample on boundary pixels (about 2–4% of pixels).
 - The projection-axis switch on mixed-material cliffs and building walls: a blend band, or a per-block axis choice made on the CPU?
--- harnessIssues
 - bench.mjs times one single-shot mesh() per chunk. For mx-codes it reported 37–56 ms mean across identical runs, while timing.mjs (interleaved min of 11) gives 14–16 ms. Its mean cannot support cost claims.
 - seams.mjs compares only per-vertex data, so fragment-side data (per-block codes) needs a separate test. I added work/mx/seamcodes.mjs, plus a test-only per-vertex code digest (MX_SEAMDIGEST=1) so seams.mjs at least covers codes next to the shared plane. My test is the more sensitive one: it catches apron 9 at Wa 4, which seams.mjs misses.
 - No scene exercises the projection-axis switch on steep mixed-material terrain; I found the tear visually on bench chunk (−1,1,1). No metric scores what riser fragments show on slopes, or material balance on real terrain.
 - Round 1's mx-contour computed codes lazily inside fragment(), so its run, bench and timing numbers excluded the code pass. mx-codes replaces it; the compare table keeps mx-contour only for reference.
 - The fragment contract returns a hard material id and there is no GPU in this environment, so per-fragment cost and the skip bit's benefit can only be logged as load and op counts (work/mx/stats).

--- production
 dataPath: As prototyped and validated in WGSL (layout A):
- CPU, after the hints mesh: codes for the surface blocks a fragment can reach (in-plane [−1, 32], axis [−3, 34]), about 171 per terrain chunk (max 336), 8 B each.
- Per chunk, one r32uint (32 + 2·3)³ = 38³ volume (214 KB): material 3 bits, sharp 1, exposure 6, has-code(d) 6, code base 16.
- Codes in array<vec2u>, ordered by texel and then direction (1.3 KB average).
- Fragment: chunk-local position as an extra vec3f varying (exact, from pos_ao); projection axis from the interpolated normal; column search 1–3 texel loads; if has-code(d), index = base + popcount(mask below d), one 8-byte load, ≤ 2 polyline side tests.
- Measured loads per fragment: mountain view 1.35, beach view 1.34. Codes are read by 15–17% of fragments.
- With the per-vertex skip bit (an interpolated 0/1 at flags bit 2), 30% / 46% of fragments read nothing and the mean drops to 1.00 / 0.78 loads.
- Memory at R = 192: 42 MiB (200 meshed chunks) to 84 MiB (400, the budget proxy). Too much; the layout below fixes that.

Recommended production layout (B, designed and sized, not built):
- 16-bit texels for the chunk's own 32³ (material, sharp, exposure, has-code), 64 KB.
- A per-chunk open-addressing hash of codes keyed by (cell·8 + d), about 6 KB, ≈1.2 probes of 12–16 B.
- A 27-entry neighbour-slot table (108 B), so border columns read the neighbour chunk's texels and codes. Those are seam-identical, as verified.
- All of it lives in pooled storage buffers, with offsets stored next to chunk_origins[slot].
- Total 70 KB per chunk: 14–27 MiB at R = 192 (an 8-bit variant is 7–15 MiB at about one extra load).
- Only own blocks are coded (about 138 per chunk), which allows apron 9.
- About 28 chunks fit the 2 MB-per-frame streaming upload cap.

What can move to per-vertex attributes: only the skip bit. The surface-block lookup is per column under each pixel, not per vertex.
 vertexFormat: The 16-byte vertex is unchanged. It uses flags bit 2 for the skip bit, interpolated as a float. Material stays the quad's own (flat), and the 3 reserved bytes stay free, so they conflict neither with mf-iso's use of them nor with anything else.

Extra varyings: the chunk-local position (vec3f, rebuilt exactly from pos_ao in the vertex shader) and the skip float, 4 interpolants in total. No new per-vertex data beyond 1 bit.
 determinism: Stored values (codes, texels, skip bits) use only + − × ÷, Math.sqrt / round / floor / abs / min / max (the generator's allowlist), comparisons and deterministic loop and sort orders, all in f64. I audited prod.mjs: no hypot, pow, ** or trig.
- Crack fits are canonical, done once in the owner's or high cell's window.
- Boundary points on cell edges are computed in a canonical edge frame from exact (half-integer + τ) forms, so the two cells sharing an edge quantise bit-identical points.
- Output checksum over 4,549 codes is reproducible: 3fe2b2a4.
- Seams: 0 differing codes and 0 of 93,061 differing fragments over 11 chunk pairs (x, z and y neighbours, real terrain plus a painted and built synthetic world). The official seams.mjs with a code digest shows 0 of 160 vertices mismatched.
- Fragment math is f32 (exempt). WGSL equals JS on 450,000 samples.
 apron: 10 for the prototyped layout, which is the existing 52³ edited-path region. Reach:
- in-plane: block −1, owner −2, window ±7, so −9;
- axis: block −3, owner ±2, window ±2, exposure ±1, so −9;
- region valid range [−9, 40].
The bound holds by construction at the final Wa 2, and 0 clipped searches occurred in 30 real chunks. Layout B (own blocks only) needs apron 9 (50³).

Cost of the apron: today's unedited GEN_MESH path generates 38³ in 3.1 ms; 52³ costs 7.4 ms (50³: 6.9).
 cpuCost: All numbers are interleaved min-of-N on this shared machine, JS prototype.
- Code pass: 5.0 ms per terrain chunk (prep 0.56, scan 0.69, window fill 1.26, own-crack fits 0.66, assembly 1.71, texels 0.14) and 7.9 ms per edited chunk. Skip bits add 0.97 ms.
- timing.mjs, mesh only at apron 10: hints 9.91 / 10.87 / 15.18 ms (mean / median / max), mx-codes 16.05 / 14.15 / 25.44 ms. That is +6.1 ms mean (+62%), +3.3 median, +10.3 worst.
- Unedited streaming per chunk: today 8.8 ms (38³ generation + mesh). mx needs 7.4 ms generation at 52³ + 5.6 ms mesh on the 38³ core + about 6 ms codes and skip, ≈ 19 ms (+115%), or ≈ 24 ms if the mesher runs on all of 52³. That roughly doubles initial-fill worker time (PLAN: 3–5 s at R = 192).
- Edit path (REMESH 52³, editbench min of 9): mountain 16.1 → 28.0 ms, single block 6.3 → 13.5, town 9.3 → 18.7. For the corner edit the worst of 8 chunks goes 10.6 → 14.1 ms (sum 53 → 90). That adds about 3.5 ms to the C-12 critical path with 8 parallel jobs, about 7 ms with 2 rounds on 4 workers.
- Not implemented: a global per-direction image pass for heightfield regions to replace per-block windows (estimated 3–5×), incremental code updates per edit (only blocks within ±8 of the edit), and a TypeScript version inside the mesher sharing its arrays.
 gpuCost: Not measured on a GPU. Dawn here only exposes llvmpipe; real Windows Chrome timing was not run unattended.

Counted from mx.wgsl:
- uncoded path: about 50–55 scalar ops + 1 texel load (+16 ops and 1 load per extra search step; 70% of fragments need 1 load, 25% need 2, 5% need 3);
- coded path: about 105–150 ops + 2 loads;
- terrain mix about 66 ops + 1.35 dependent 4-byte loads per fragment, or about 40–45 ops + 0.8–1.0 loads with the skip bit;
- 4 extra interpolants; no extra triangles (+0%).

Estimate for the i7-1255U Iris Xe at 2.1 MP (about 2.7 M shaded fragments per frame): +0.3–0.7 ms per frame, about 3–6% of the 12 ms Medium GPU budget. The main risk is latency from the dependent texel → code chain and divergence at boundaries. This estimate is the main open risk and needs a real-GPU measurement.
 physics: None. Geometry, collision and picking are the hints mesh, unchanged: every geometry and constraint row equals hints. Only the displayed material changes; what you see is what you hit still holds.
 editability: Edits stay local:
- A code depends on blocks within ±8 in-plane and ±5 along the axis, inside the existing dirty set [b − 9, b + 10] (PLAN §7.5), so an edit dirties no extra chunks.
- Repainting a block's material updates nearby boundaries smoothly within about 8 blocks.
- Sharp blocks are excluded (treated as unknown) and stay crisp, which is the opt-out for crisp patterns.
- Checkers of 4×4 or more and square corners stay crisp even with smooth blocks.
- Edit latency: +7–12 ms per dirty chunk in this JS prototype (see cpuCost).
--- combinesWith
 Every sheet is three columns: left hints (grey strip), middle mf-iso (orange), right mx-codes (blue).

Geometry and AO: mx-codes reads only block data, the interpolated normal and the chunk-local position. It composes with any geometry or AO rule that keeps fragments within ±2 levels of their block surface: gn-best AO, gs-ctx/gs-mls and gh-cc8 built-geometry rules, or plain hints. It needs no per-corner vertices and no quad structure, so retriangulation is fine.

Against mf-iso, head to head:
- mx-codes wins on shallow edges (1:8 0.042 vs 0.131; 1:5 0.041 vs 0.054), the 1:2 line (0.007 vs 0.043), line width (ideal vs ×1.34), square corners, the checker, the lone block (×0.79 round dot vs ×0.56), the junction (0.034 vs 0.085), the hill (0.131 vs 0.142), and built walls (no stone creeping up brick, sheet built).
- mf-iso wins on the larger discs (0.129 / 0.148 vs 0.130 / 0.163), the 1:3 edge, the 2-wide band and generator dither (one blob). It also costs about 1 ms less CPU, needs no GPU tables or extra loads, and uses apron 6.
- They are alternatives: both claim the same material appearance, and mf-iso uses the spare vertex bits that mx leaves free.

A sensible hybrid: mx-codes for player-edited content (exact lines and crisp corners), with the survey's generator material hints, or mf-iso, for generated terrain. A per-block 'generated' bit in the texel would choose between them.

The crack-fit stage could also take τ directly from continuous generator fields for unedited cells, giving exact generated boundaries through the same data path.
