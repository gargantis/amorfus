# v-local

The combined claim is only partly true. A local rule exists that bounds its reach to apron 2 and keeps small features. It does not reproduce the FID-0.15 physics, it fails two of the four game constraints on its own, and its vertex solver misplaces about 3.8 % of terrain corners.

What holds:
- **Locality, by construction and measured.** One block edited changes the b1 field at most 2.43 blocks away (2.65 for the Gaussian version). The vertex rule changes only corners within 1 block of the edit. Apron 2 gives 0 seam mismatches; apron 1 does not.
- **Exact corner locality of the b1 kernel.** At sigma ≤ 0.204 a corner's field depends only on its 8 blocks.
- **Volume.** On 15 configurations outside the fitted catalogue, the local-lambda volume error is within ±4 % per material (worst cluster 5.9 %, a 1-block cluster).
- **Physics checks of other teams.** Local's L1 and L2 reproduce, the far terrace jump scaling as 1/n (−1/12 at n12, −1/16 at n16). Look's far-bar result reproduces exactly.

What breaks:
- **The far-bar effect is drainage.** Under FID-0.15, a 1×1×4 bar attached to the tunnel host keeps 0.14 of its 4 block³, and the tunnel fills 5.40 of 6. The cheap model keeps both the bar and the tunnel, so per-configuration surface error reaches p95 1.0. In other words, the physics deletes thin features when the cluster has a concave sink, and a local rule cannot.
- **Tunnel clearance.** The sigma-0.2 field itself fails the 0.70 × 1.90 clear-height test (1.75–1.785), not only the vertex rule (mesh 1.73–1.75). Only a doorway through a 1-thick wall passes.
- **Flat ground.** It never stays at its height: +3/256 on thick ground, +6/256 on each face of a 1-thick floor, −9/256 to +8/256 on mixed-material ground.
- **Vertex solver.** Fast's "0.4 % Newton failures" measured self-consistency (4 vs 16 iterations, reproduced as 65 of 17,284 corners), not correctness. Both solvers leave 3.7–3.8 % of terrain corners off their own surface, although every one of those corners has a surface inside its box. The line solver misplaces 461 of 616 seven-solid two-material corners.
- **FINITE-T scaling.** Sigma·√T matches the best mean at T = 8 (0.55 against 0.566 predicted) and T = 16 (0.80). The one-step error doubles with each doubling of T, and multi-material and single-material configurations want different sigma.

Nothing was written outside teams/v-local, and no process of mine was left running. The folder is backed up.

## new findings
- FID-0.15's far-bar non-locality is mass transfer. A thin same-cluster feature drains completely into a concavity ≥ 3 blocks away: an attached 1×1×4 bar empties and fills a 1×2 tunnel. FID-0.25 does not do this, and neither does any configuration without a concave sink. So FID-0.15 does not keep thin features in general, and the cheap local model, which keeps them, departs from the physics exactly here.
- The b1 vertex rule leaves 3.7–3.8 % of generated-terrain corners off its own D = 0 surface, although every such corner has a surface inside its clamp box. The line solver fails 75 % of 7-solid two-material corners (grass/dirt seams): 461 of 616. Displacements reach 0.59 (line) and 1.16 (Newton). The defect shows on terrain as dirt showing through at seams: 34 of 230 corners in one window. Fast's 0.4 % figure measured only agreement between 4 and 16 Newton iterations.
- Any sigma-0.2 blur model fails the 0.70 × 1.90 tunnel window before any vertex rule is applied. The four concave edges of a 1×2 hole fill by about 0.075 at 0.15 from the wall, giving clear height 1.85 at lambda 0 and 1.75–1.785 with the local lambda. A clearance cap after the rule is needed regardless of solver.
- Flat ground is never left at its height by the cheap rule, unlike the repo's bevel rule. Uniform thick ground rises 3/256. A 1-thick floor thickens by 6/256 on each face. Mixed-material ground ripples −9/256 to +8/256: material seams form grooves 0.03–0.035 deep.
- The FID-0.15 reference tunnel is resolution-dependent: clear height at width 0.70 is 1.21 at n12 and 0.87 at n16 (margin 0.75), and mid-height 1.50 against 1.13. Accuracy figures quoted 'against FID-0.15' at a single n carry this uncertainty.
- Under FINITE-4, two pits superpose badly (D0 residual 0.204) even though each single pit leaves lambda unchanged (0.266): only the pair moves it, to 0.2223. Lambda pinning cannot be judged per feature.
- Under FID-0.15 with volume off, a 1-thick membrane framed on all sides is stable, and punctures of 1–9 blocks stay local (≤ 0.21 block). Free-edged 1-thick plates vanish. Without volume, losing thin features is an edge effect, not a cascade.
## open
- Is FID-0.15's drainage of thin features into distant concavities wanted bubble behaviour for the game, or a regime to avoid (FID-0.25 does not do it)? This is the owner's look decision.
- Can a corrected vertex solver place the 7-solid two-material corners on their surface at acceptable cost, for example a nearest-zero search or an offline table over corner patterns? Not built or timed. About 743 corner-only and diagonal-saddle corners cannot be fixed by any single-vertex placement (inference).
- Cross-browser determinism of the vertex rule was not tested. By code reading the b1 path uses only + - * /, comparisons, clamps, Math.round and one Math.sqrt at set-up, all exactly rounded in IEEE double (inference).
- The FID-0.15 reference is not converged in n (tunnel 1.21 at n12, 0.87 at n16). Which resolution should accuracy claims use?
- Clearances were measured on the vertex positions and the PLAN 7.2 quad mesh. Fast's C11 shows on-surface vertices with flat quads differ from the field. A quad-level clearance cap after the rule was not tested.
