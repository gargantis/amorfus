# TEAM prior

I checked about 50 sources online. The verification log, with URLs and what each check confirmed, is teams/prior/citations-log.md; UNVERIFIED items are marked there and nothing relies on them. In literature terms the model is a multiphase minimal partition with an air reservoir: same material means zero tension, so one phase; different materials meet across a film of tension g; equilibria obey Plateau's laws (Plateau 1873, Taylor 1976, Morgan 1994). The engine reproduces the Young/Neumann closed form for a 2D double bubble: wall within 0.004 blocks and tip within 0.021 blocks at n=64, for g = 0.5, 1 and 1.5. A block lattice is almost never a Plateau configuration. Of 738 corner classes, only 85 pass Plateau's necessary checks and 650 have a four-film lattice line, so on blocks bubble physics is first a set of T1/pop decisions. The lower-tension diagonal wins; ties are common, and the owner's painted diagonal line is a chain of ties that physics does not decide. The persistent four-way junction in the README has an exact cause: with equal tensions the Gaussian-thresholded axis-aligned cross is a fixed point (0 changes within 1 block over 5 steps), and a T1 nucleus of 2 sub-cells or less collapses back. The engine's jitter is frozen per cell, phase and seed. Re-drawing it every step (auction dynamics' 'temperature', Jacobs et al. 2018) at 0.1 for 100 steps, then quenching, resolved the junction in 5/5 2D and 3/3 3D runs; frozen or 1e-2 noise never did. Pinning (Esedoglu-Ruuth-Tsai; Misiats-Yip 2016) is real but narrower than the README's half-sub-cell rule: plain-MBO disks shrink at the theoretical rate up to R=2 at n=16 and R=6 at n=32. The FID regime is TV-L1 (Chan-Esedoglu 2005): without volume the corner radius follows r = s/(k*sqrt(2*pi)) at 82-95 %, but with volume rounding nearly vanishes and k=0.15 oscillates. Volume preservation makes the model non-local (106 cells differ 4 blocks from an edit after 6 steps, 0 without volume). Seam grooves are Mullins thermal grooves with opening angle 2*acos(g/2). - [corner-classes] (pattern, measured) A 2x2x2 lattice corner has 21,147 equality patterns (materials compared only for equality; air distinguished), falling into 738 classes under the 48 cube symmetries. Only 85 classes pass Plateau's two necessary checks: no lattice line with four films around it, and at most 4 regions meeting at the point. 650 classes contain a four-film line and 280 have more than 4 regions. Restricted to surface corners with at most 2 solid materials: 132 classes, 92 with a four-film line, 49 of them with a tension tie. Binary solid/air (today's mesher): 20 classes; 9 have a four-film line, all ties, and these are PLAN 7.2's pinched diagonal-only contacts. Under bubble physics most corners therefore need a topological decision (T1/pop, as in the Surface Evolver's 'O'/'o'/'pop' commands) before any smoothing.
  numbers: 738 classes / 21147 labelled; 85 Plateau-passing; 650 with X-line; 280 >4 regions; surface<=2 solids 132/92/49; binary 20 classes, 9 with X-line; orchestrator cross-check 738/551/22
  refuteBy: Enumerate independently and get different counts. Or anneal and quench a configuration (several seeds) and show that a four-film line, or a point where more than 4 regions meet, survives as a stable equilibrium.
  evidence: teams/prior/corner-classes.json, teams/prior/corner-classes.mjs, teams/prior/orch-classes-crosscheck.log, teams/prior/citations-log.md

- [x-rule-per-edge] (pattern, inferred) A four-way (X) junction on a lattice edge with unit outer films at 90 degrees, split by a central film of tension tau and length L, changes energy by (tau - sqrt2)*L to first order. The outer-film term is the same for both splits, so the diagonal pair with the lower film tension gets the central film. Engine tensions: same material 0 (unites), air-air 0, solid-air 1, solid-solid g. Decided cases: ABAC and ABA-air (the A pair unites), air-A-air-B (A and B stay apart). Ties: ABAB (checkerboard), A-air-A-air (diagonal-only contact), ABCD, plus ABC-air at the default g=1. The decision lives on the 4 blocks around one lattice edge: 52 labelled patterns, 17 classes, 7 with an X, 3 tied at generic g and 4 at g=1. The owner's painted 1-wide diagonal line of A in B is a chain of AB/BA ties, so physics does not decide whether it connects; a tie policy must (cf. the asymptotic decider, Nielson-Hamann 1991; (8,4) connectivity, Kong-Rosenfeld 1989).
  numbers: dE = (tau - 1.414) L; per-edge table 52 patterns / 17 classes / 7 X / 3 ties (4 at g=1)
  refuteBy: Run 2D FREE on ABA-air or ABAC 2x2 configurations with annealed noise 0.1 and a quench, over 10 seeds. If the higher-tension diagonal (B-air or B-C) gets the central wall in most seeds, the rule is wrong.
  evidence: teams/prior/corner-classes.json, teams/prior/corner-classes.mjs

- [x-fixed-point] (limitation, measured) With equal tensions among the four phases, the axis-aligned four-phase cross is an exact fixed point of the engine's Gaussian threshold step. The blurred quadrant indicators are products Phi(x/s)Phi(y/s), so each cell's lowest cost is its own quadrant. This is the cause of README limit 2 (a persistent X), rather than generic pinning. No paper found states this for threshold dynamics; the derivation is this team's.
  numbers: 0 label changes within 1 block of the cross over 5 steps, for g=1 and g=0.5, with and without the volume solve (n=32, four 2x2-block quadrants)
  refuteBy: With jitter 0 and equal tensions, find any label change within 1 block of an axis-aligned four-phase cross.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-xfixed.json, teams/prior/xfixed.mjs, teams/prior/citations-log.md

- [t1-nucleus-critical] (limitation, measured) In 2D sq2-ABCD FREE (n=32, s=6.4 sub-cells, jitter 0), a seeded A-D T1 nucleus of radius 1 or 2 sub-cells collapses back to the X. Radius 3 to 16 survives, but the final wall freezes at a length that depends on the seed instead of reaching one equilibrium. So the X is stable at sub-kernel scale, and walls are pinned once formed.
  numbers: r=1,2: X restored; r=3,4,6,8,12,16: final A-D contact 2,6,12,12,14,16 face-adjacent sub-cell pairs
  refuteBy: Rerun xjunc.mjs presplit. A wall surviving at r<=2, or final wall lengths that do not depend on r (one equilibrium), refutes it.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-presplit.json, teams/prior/presplit.log, teams/prior/img/sheet-x2d.png

- [annealed-noise-fix] (engine, measured) The engine's jitter is a frozen function of (cell, phase, seed). Re-drawing it every step, as in auction dynamics' temperature (Jacobs-Merkurjev-Esedoglu 2018, sec. 3.4), at amplitude 0.1 for 100 steps followed by a jitter-0 quench resolves the four-material X in 2D and the four-film line in 3D. Frozen noise at 1e-3 or 1e-2, and re-drawn noise at 1e-2, do not.
  numbers: 2D: frozen 1e-3 and 1e-2 X persists 6/6; annealed 1e-2 3/3 persist; anneal 0.03 x100 + quench 1/5 resolved; anneal 0.1 x100 + quench 5/5 resolved (1 A-D, 4 B-C). 3D (n=16): persists at jitter 0, frozen 1e-3, annealed 1e-2; anneal 0.1 + quench 3/3 resolved (seed 2 one B-C wall 0.719 blocks^2; seeds 1 and 3 both walls ~0.74-0.80 blocks^2)
  refuteBy: Run anneal 0.1 + quench on seeds 6-20. Persisting Xs in more than a few seeds refute it. So does frozen jitter of amplitude 0.1 resolving the X equally often, which would show that re-drawing is not what matters.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-noise.json, teams/prior/results-anneal.json, teams/prior/results-abcd3d.json, teams/prior/results-abcd3d-anneal.json, teams/prior/img/sheet-x2d.png, teams/prior/img/sheet-x3d.png

- [young-neumann-engine] (physics, measured) For the 2D equal double bubble with A-B tension g, the engine (FREE, measured on labels) matches the Young/Neumann closed form: R^2[pi - acos(g/2) + (g/2)sqrt(1-g^2/4)] = 1, wall half-length R*sqrt(1-g^2/4), wall-to-tip R(1+g/2).
  numbers: n=64: wall half 0.6719/0.5469/0.3906 vs theory 0.6737/0.5447/0.3874 (g=0.5/1/1.5); tips 0.8906/0.9531/1.0313 vs 0.8697/0.9435/1.025. n=32 errors up to 0.049 (wall, g=0.5) and 0.068 (tip, g=0.5)
  refuteBy: Rerun at n=128. Diverging from the closed form refutes it, as does an independent solver (e.g. Surface Evolver string model) giving different junction geometry for g=0.5.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-neumann.json, teams/prior/img/sheet-neumann.png

- [seam-groove] (look, partly-measured) Where an A-B wall meets air, Young's balance (Mullins 1957, thermal grooving) sets the opening angle of the air surface at 2*acos(g/2): 120 deg at g=1, 151 deg at g=0.5, 83 deg at g=1.5. So the grooves at different-material seams in the orchestrator's FID images are the expected geometry, not an artefact. A small g (<=0.25 gives >=166 deg) nearly removes the groove, but the same tension is what straightens a jagged seam, so smoothness of boundaries trades off against seam flatness (inference).
  numbers: opening 151.0 / 120 / 82.8 deg (theory, results-neumann.json); junction geometry matched at n=64 (claim young-neumann-engine)
  refuteBy: Measure the groove angle on labels in 3D FID seam runs at g in {0.25, 1, 1.5}. An angle that does not depend on g, or does not follow 2acos(g/2) within the resolution, refutes it.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-neumann.json, teams/prior/citations-log.md

- [fid-is-tvl1] (physics, measured) The FID-k regime is the TV-L1 / flat-norm problem min Per + lambda*|symmetric difference| (Chan-Esedoglu 2005). From the engine's costs a boundary cell keeps its phase while |kappa| <= k*sqrt(2*pi)/s, so convex block corners round to r = s/(k*sqrt(2*pi)), with s in blocks and kappa the sum of principal curvatures in 3D. Without the volume solve this holds at 82-95 %. With the volume solve on (the study's FID regime) corners barely round, and k=0.15 never converges (a 2-cycle).
  numbers: 4x4 square, no volume, n=64: r = 0.754/0.481/0.217 vs predicted 0.798/0.532/0.266 for k=0.1/0.15/0.3 (n=32: 0.754/0.434/0.226). With volume: 0.321/0.217/0.009 at n=64; k=0.15 flips 336 (n=32) / 680 (n=64) cells every step for 300 steps
  refuteBy: Change s at fixed k without the volume solve; r not scaling linearly with s refutes it. Showing that the volume-on 2-cycle disappears with an exact (auction) volume solve would make that part a solver artefact, not inherent.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-fid.json, teams/prior/fid.log, teams/prior/img/sheet-fid-tvl1.png

- [pinning-disks] (limitation, partly-measured) Plain MBO (no volume, s=0.2 blocks) shrinks disks at the curve-shortening rate pi*s^2 = 0.126 blocks^2/step up to R=2 blocks at n=16 and R=6 at n=32. At n=16 it slows from R=2.25 and freezes at R=3-4. The half-sub-cell estimate R_pin ~ s^2*n (0.64 / 1.28 blocks) is at least 3.5x too pessimistic for curved boundaries. Inference: pinning in the study mostly comes from grid-aligned flats (block faces) and from volume-constrained runs near equilibrium, where the driving force is only a curvature difference. Literature: MBO 'gets stuck' (Esedoglu-Ruuth-Tsai citing MBO 1994); orientation-dependent pinning regimes (Misiats-Yip 2016).
  numbers: n=16: loss/step 0.1302 at R=2, 0.1081 (R 2.25), 0.0924 (2.5), 0.0677 (2.75), 0.0234 (3), 0 (4); n=32: >=0.1068 for all R<=6; theory 0.1257
  refuteBy: Repeat with disk centres at random sub-cell offsets. Pinning at R<=1.28 for n=32 refutes the measured part. An axis-aligned flat with small imposed curvature that moves freely refutes the inference.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-pin.json, teams/prior/pin.log, teams/prior/citations-log.md

- [sigma-stability-class] (engine, measured) The engine's tension matrix (solid-air 1, different materials g, same material separate clusters 0.05) is conditionally negative semi-definite in every tested phase set for g <= 2. That puts it inside Esedoglu-Otto's Proposition 1 class: each step dissipates the nonlocal energy, given a kernel with G >= 0 and Fourier transform >= 0. For g > 2 the triangle inequality fails, because an air film is cheaper than an A-B wall (dewetting); at g=2.5 with 22 phases the matrix also leaves the stability class.
  numbers: g in {0.1,...,2}, 3-22 phases: max projected eigenvalue <= 0; g=2.5: triangle slack -0.5, max eigenvalue +0.141 with 7 materials x 3 clusters
  refuteBy: Find a phase set with g <= 2 and eps 0.05 whose largest eigenvalue on sum-zero vectors is positive.
  evidence: teams/prior/sigma-check.json, teams/prior/sigma-check.mjs, teams/prior/citations-log.md

- [tent-kernel] (optimization, measured) A separable tent kernel (box*box, two running sums per axis) satisfies Esedoglu-Otto's kernel conditions (G >= 0, Fourier transform sinc^2 >= 0). It blurs about 2x faster than the engine's truncated Gaussian at the same scale. It is anisotropic, and the gain for a whole step was not measured.
  numbers: 96^3 = 884,736 cells, s=3.2 sub-cells: Gaussian 21 taps median 122.2 ms vs tent (box 7, variance 8) median 59.5 ms, speed-up 2.05 (runs 86-206 vs 42-92 ms; noisy)
  refuteBy: Repeat the benchmark with a variance-matched tent (box 9) and whole-step timing. A speed-up below 1.2x refutes the optimization's value.
  evidence: teams/prior/results-blurbench.json, teams/prior/blurbench.mjs

- [one-step-is-blobby] (pattern, inferred) At g=1 with one cluster per material, the engine's costs reduce to psi_i = 1 - u_i for solids and Sall = 1 - u_air for air. One unconstrained threshold step is then exactly the argmax of the per-material Gaussian-blurred block indicators, air included. That is a multi-material blobby/convolution surface (Blinn 1982; Bloomenthal-Shoemake 1991) with selective blending by group (Kacic-Alesic and Wyvill 1991, citing Beier 1990): same material blends (unites), different materials do not. At s=0.2 blocks a lone block keeps about 0.96 of its centre density, so it survives, unlike PLAN 7.7's rejected wide blur.
  numbers: psi_i = (1-Sall) + g(Sall - sm) + eps(sm - u_i) = 1 - u_i at g=1, sm=u_i; lone-block centre u ~ erf(0.5/(0.2*sqrt2))^3 ~ 0.96
  refuteBy: Run one engine step (volume false, g=1, one cluster per material) and find a cell whose new label differs from the argmax of the blurred indicators including air.
  evidence: lib/mbo.mjs, teams/prior/citations-log.md

- [power-diagram-no-smoothing] (limitation, inferred) Power-diagram foam models (Busaryev-Dey-Wang-Ren 2012) place walls on radical planes between weighted sites. With one site per block and equal weights (unit volumes) the power diagram is the Voronoi diagram of the lattice, i.e. the block faces themselves, so it adds no smoothing of material walls. It helps only at cluster level, for FREE-like spheres.

  refuteBy: Show a per-block weighting that respects the blocks' unit volumes and yields walls that are not lattice planes.

## optimizations
- Decide T1s per lattice edge with a small table over the equality pattern of the 4 blocks around the edge (52 patterns, 17 classes, 7 with an X). Give the central film to the lower-tension diagonal, break ties with an explicit policy (e.g. connect the locally minority material so painted lines stay whole; that is the owner's call), and check corners only for more than 4 regions. Keep geometry separate from this topology step.
  basis: ['Lorensen & Cline 1987 (case table)', 'Hege et al. 1997 ZIB SC-97-05 and Wu & Sullivan 2003 IJNME 58 (multi-material MC)', 'Nielson & Hamann 1991 (asymptotic decider)', 'Brakke 1992 Surface Evolver pop / t1_edgeswap', 'Kong & Rosenfeld 1989 (connectivity pairs)', 'Taylor 1976; Morgan 1994 (Plateau)', "this team's corner-classes.json and the (tau - sqrt2)L derivation"]; gain: Replaces the random noise the engine needs to escape X junctions with a deterministic, local, integer decision that fits the golden-hash rule. The table is tiny (52 entries per edge; 21,147 / 738 classes if indexed per corner).; risks: The tie policy is taste, not physics. Decisions on the edges of one corner must be mutually consistent. A tie-break that favours thin painted lines may fill dither and checker patterns.
- Junction-line fairing as in Frisken 2022: vertices on lines where three or more phases meet (the A|B|air seams on the surface) are smoothed along the line only, other vertices over the surface, all inside a cell-bounded range (the repo's 7.4 constraint box).
  basis: ['Frisken 2022 JCGT 11(1) (EdgeVertex vs SurfaceVertex fairing, AllowedRange)', 'Gibson 1998 MICCAI (constrained surface nets)', 'Plateau-border / triple-line straightening by wall tension (Young; Mullins 1957)']; gain: Straight, smooth material seams on the surface at surface-nets cost, local and deterministic: the closest existing algorithm to 'smoothness of boundaries'.; risks: Does not decide X junctions (needs the per-edge table). Frisken uses one vertex per cell, while amorfus splits vertices per (corner, material), so it must be adapted. Painted lines still need the tie policy.
- Set the FID strength from the TV-L1 scale law: without volume, convex corners round to r = s/(k*sqrt(2*pi)); features below radius 2/lambda_eff (lambda_eff = k*sqrt(2*pi)/s) vanish in the global minimizer.
  basis: ['Chan & Esedoglu 2005 SIAM J. Appl. Math. 65 (TV-L1 geometry, disk threshold 2/r)', 'results-fid.json (82-95 % agreement)']; gain: Pick k for a target rounding radius and predict which features disappear, without parameter sweeps.; risks: The volume solve breaks the law (rounding suppressed; k=0.15 oscillates). Without volume a lone block may vanish at k=0.15 (inferred, not run). 3D corners follow sum-of-curvatures, so a sphere corner rounds twice as wide as a cylinder edge.
- Replace the truncated Gaussian blur by a separable tent kernel (box*box via running sums), or design the anisotropy deliberately to keep a blocky-but-soft look.
  basis: ['Esedoglu & Otto 2015 CPAM 68, condition (49): G >= 0 and Fourier transform >= 0', 'Esedoglu, Jacobs & Zhang 2017 JCP (kernel to anisotropic tension)', 'Ishii, Pires & Souganidis 1999 (general kernels)', 'Kacic-Alesic & Wyvill 1991 (compact radius of influence)', 'results-blurbench.json']; gain: Measured 2.05x faster blur at s=3.2 on 885k cells (blur only); compact support by construction.; risks: Anisotropic surface tension changes the look. Whole-step speed-up not measured; the volume solve and assignment may dominate. Timings were noisy.
- Budget locality explicitly: NOVOL-T reaches T*0.625 blocks, so T<=4 fits the 3-block apron. Volume-preserving regimes need a global per-cluster pressure computed outside the chunk mesh job.
  basis: ['Young-Laplace (one pressure per bubble)', 'results-locality.json', 'lib/mbo.mjs gaussKernel truncation at ceil(3s)']; gain: Seam-safe chunk meshing with a known apron.; risks: NOVOL is mean-curvature flow: small features shrink and vanish (PLAN 7.4 guards apply). Computing a global pressure per cluster does not fit the current per-chunk job model.
- In the research engine only: re-draw the jitter every step (temperature), then quench, to escape symmetric X junctions and local minima.
  basis: ['Jacobs, Merkurjev & Esedoglu 2018 JCP 354, sec. 3.4 (temperature; Weaire-Phelan from a shifted cubic lattice)', 'results-anneal.json, results-abcd3d-anneal.json']; gain: X resolved in 5/5 (2D) and 3/3 (3D) runs, where frozen noise failed in 6/6.; risks: Random, so not for the game. Results vary with seed (A-D vs B-C wall; one wall vs both).
- Use a sub-cell representation in the research engine (signed distance, or area fractions interpolated from psi - lambda) to remove pinning from study results.
  basis: ['Esedoglu, Ruuth & Tsai 2010 JCP 229', 'Ruuth 1998 JCP 144', 'Jacobs et al. 2018 sec. 4 sub-pixel trick', 'Misiats & Yip 2016']; gain: Equilibrium shapes that do not depend on the seed (fixes the frozen T1 wall lengths and part of the Neumann error at n=32).; risks: Improves the research, not the game. Signed-distance variants need redistancing every step.
- Power-diagram walls (radical planes) for bubble walls.
  basis: ['Busaryev, Dey, Wang & Ren 2012 TOG 31(4)']; gain: Closed-form walls for cluster-level spheres (FREE-like scenes only).; risks: With equal block weights the power diagram is the block grid, so no smoothing of material walls. Low value for amorfus.
## open
- Tie policy for A B / B A lattice edges (the owner's painted diagonal line): should the line or the background connect? Physics does not decide; prior art offers only conventions ((8,4) connectivity, the asymptotic decider).
- Should volume conservation be part of any game rule at all? It makes the model non-local (106 cells changed 4 blocks from an edit) and suppresses or destabilises FID rounding.
- Is the FID + volume 2-cycle (k=0.15, 336/680 cells flipping every step) an artefact of the engine's Jacobi quantile volume solve or inherent? Test with an exact auction-dynamics volume solve on the same 4x4 square.
- In 3D sq2-ABCD, which has lower energy: the pairwise-touching (tetrahedral-cone) cluster of seeds 1 and 3, or the single wall of seed 2? A Surface Evolver run would settle it.
- How strong is pinning on grid-aligned flat faces and on volume-constrained shapes near equilibrium, which the disk test does not cover?
- What A-B tension g gives the preferred balance between seam groove depth (opening angle 2acos(g/2)) and seam straightening? This needs a 3D seam test across g under FID.
